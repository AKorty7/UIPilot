# Soft Signal

*A design philosophy for UIPilot's store art, written with the canvas-design skill.*

**Soft Signal** treats light as the one material. A world is never shown directly. It is
seen through frosted glass, a veil of periwinkle or midnight that softens every edge until
only what glows can pass through. Form is what survives the veil: a horizon, a floor that
runs to a single vanishing point, a few pearl bodies resting on it, one ring standing at
the edge of sight. Nothing is decorated. Everything is lit.

Space is organised like a calm instrument. A perspective grid of hairlines, each one ruled
with painstaking precision, measures the ground from the viewer to the horizon. The lines
thin and fade with distance at an exact rate, so depth reads without a single shadow being
overstated. Objects sit on that grid the way readings sit on a scale: placed, spaced and
measured, never scattered. The composition leaves the left of the frame quiet and open,
and lets the right hold the event. That imbalance is deliberate, a stage kept clear.

Colour comes in three states of one sky. Day is an airy wash from periwinkle to aqua to
mint, with a white bloom where the light source sits. Night keeps the same world and turns
it down to navy and violet, so only emissive things remain: cyan rules, pinpoint stars, the
rim of a sphere. Ink is the world at dusk, desaturated to slate, lit by one thin teal line
at the horizon. The three share a single geometry, the way one room looks at three hours
of the day.

Detail lives at the scale of hairlines and grain. Registration marks, one-pixel frames and
a whisper of film grain give the surfaces the density of a meticulously crafted print.
Gradients are long and never banded. Blooms fall off smoothly, and every radius is chosen,
not guessed. The work should look labored over by a master of the form: the product of
countless small corrections, where nothing is accidental and every element could be
defended.

Among the pearls, one is singled out by a thin square frame with crosshair corners. It is
the only mark of intention in a still world, the quiet sign that something here has been
selected, reached and found in working order. Text, where it appears at all, is wide,
thin and lowercase. It is placed like a label on an instrument, never a sentence. The
finished pieces should feel like artefacts from a careful laboratory of light: calm,
exact and unmistakably made with deep expertise.
